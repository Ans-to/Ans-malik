# Composable storyboard renderer — capabilities and limits

`python ceo_render_app.py --topic "Why Your AI Gives You Wrong Answers" --duration 15 --output renders/final_video.mp4` compiles a variable number of beats and renders Chromium/GSAP frames at 60 fps. Reviewed examples cover the dollar and AI topics. Other topics are compiled without a JSON file using a **non-factual overview fallback** and offline icon-name matching. The fallback repeats generic phrasing and is explicitly marked `semantic_review_required`; it is not an informative documentary script generator. For substantive narration or custom actors, provide a reviewed `--storyboard path.json`. Arbitrary prompts, external TTS/network failures, and unlimited durations cannot be guaranteed never to fail.

The installed `@iconify-json/lucide` and `@iconify-json/mdi` packages provide 9,764 direct icon records and 6,608 alias records (16,372 names at the versions in the lockfile). These are not 16,372 distinct illustrations. The resolver uses exact names, a small synonym dictionary and conservative lexical similarity; uncertain or missing results are recorded in `icon_matches`. A name match does not prove a pictured object represents the intended noun. Icons are loaded from the installed trusted packages and embedded only for selected actors. Their license information is included in the packages.

The stage adds a tactile paper-to-dark tear, layered bento panels, circuit paths, staggered depth, 1.45× duplicated-vector loupe, perspective tilt, SVG callouts and foreground bokeh. Ten named rig types can be selected in a storyboard (`editorial-strike`, `vault-unlock`, `sever-link`, `probability-wheel`, `ledger-cascade`, `network-hub`, `dominance-ring`, `bento-compare`, `multiply-press`, `split-paradox`). They are stylized, primarily SVG/2D approximations with CSS perspective, not a complete physics simulator or a guarantee that every noun and verb is illustrated literally. Dominance rings require an explicit numerical `value`; no statistic is invented.

Each beat uses a separately synthesized Edge Andrew Neural clip and its WordBoundary event. `acoustic_onset.py` inspects a ±120/150 ms window of the decoded PCM with SciPy STFT spectral flux and the RMS derivative. A nearby rising-energy peak is stored as a **candidate**, with its offset, evidence and fallback status in `timeline.json`. QA recomputes the result from the saved lossless `.speech.flac` file. This is not proof of word onset: peaks may be adjacent syllables, noise or vowels, and weak consonants may have no energy peak. A low-evidence case retains the Edge event and is reported in the audit rather than asserting a nonexistent onset. Actions begin at the first video frame at or after the candidate (up to one 60-fps frame later). Neither this detector nor TTS event metadata certifies sample-exact phoneme alignment.

The audio mix applies warm Pedalboard processing, targets -14 LUFS, and schedules Foley at each candidate. Foley is limited to 18 dB below voice in whole-program RMS before mastering, not necessarily per-hit peak level. Short scripts are padded with distributed silence; speech longer than the requested duration extends the output rather than changing its speed.

`python qa_inspector.py --literal VIDEO TIMELINE SHEET` verifies trigger frame arithmetic, recomputes acoustic evidence, decodes every video frame for the 98%/0.65 motion threshold and no-static-three-frame gate, and produces a labeled beat sheet. The whole-frame metric includes moving background and camera and does not prove every actor moves or every scene is semantically correct. Visual/editorial review is still required. The original legacy functions in `ceo_render_app.py` remain for compatibility; its CLI delegates to this renderer.
