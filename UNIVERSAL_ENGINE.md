# Literal storyboard renderer (bounded prototype)

`python ceo_render_app.py --topic "Why Your AI Gives You Wrong Answers" --duration 15 --output renders/final_video.mp4` compiles a variable-length list of beats and renders each frame in Chromium at 60 fps. Two built-in example plans cover the requested dollar and AI topics. The examples select approximately one beat per three requested seconds (5/10/20 beats for 15/30/60 seconds). To render another topic, provide `--storyboard path.json` in the same format as `storyboard_examples/*.json`. There is no general language-model script generator configured: unknown topics without a supplied plan fail explicitly, and built-in examples contain at most 20 unique beats.

Each beat must name exactly two supported actor types, a supported action, one trigger token actually present in the narration, and a mood. The actors are illustrative SVG rigs, not a universal 3D asset library. Validation prevents unsupported actors/actions and ambiguous/missing trigger events but cannot prove that all nouns in free-form narration are literally represented or that an arbitrary action is semantically appropriate. Extend the actor and action library or supply a reviewed storyboard rather than treating it as a semantic guarantee. The older programmatic legacy rendering functions in `ceo_render_app.py` remain for compatibility; the CLI delegates to the new engine.

Each clause is synthesized separately using Edge Andrew Neural at +0%, with WordBoundary events. Decoded PCM samples determine clip starts and durations; the matching service event determines the trigger. `timeline.json` contains integer sample positions and the corresponding millisecond values. An Edge event is an engine-reported timing, not an independently verified phoneme onset; MP3 decoding and AAC output also preclude a claim of sample-exact acoustic synchronization. Video actions start on the first 60-fps frame at or after that event, so they can start up to one frame later. With short scripts the engine distributes added silence to fill the requested duration; if natural speech exceeds it, the video is extended rather than changing speech speed. The 180ms gap is a minimum, not always the final gap.

The mix uses warm Pedalboard processing, PyLoudNorm targeting -14 LUFS and trigger-scheduled Foley constrained to at least 18 dB below voice in whole-program RMS before mastering. Loudness after limiting/AAC may vary. QA checks the actual encoded frames against the 98%/0.65 and no-static-three-frame gate, as well as no exact duplicate pairs. This is a whole-frame metric, not proof that every prop is moving or every rendered action matches its noun; moving camera and background layers contribute. The 18 dB whole-program RMS figure is not a per-hit peak or loudness guarantee. The renderer checks trigger state for each captured frame, not acoustic alignment.

For each output, `python qa_inspector.py --literal VIDEO TIMELINE SHEET` verifies metadata, checks the decoded MP4's motion, and samples every beat. Sample review is required; automated motion/trigger tests do not establish artistic quality or factual correctness. The gold example describes the 1971 suspension of official conversion; it does not claim that 88% of global trade is conducted in dollars.
